How big is the microdrama business, and how fast is it growing? The question sounds simple, but the answers in circulation range across billions of dollars, because different firms count different things. In this article we set out what three published sources say as of November 2026, explain which numbers can and cannot be compared, and show the arithmetic behind every figure we derive. Each number links to where we read it, and each one is also in our open [market figures dataset]({{ site.baseurl }}/data/), quoted word for word with its source and the date we checked it.

## Downloads are growing much faster than revenue

Sensor Tower measured more than **850 million** short drama app downloads worldwide in Q1 2026, **up 140%** year on year. In-app purchase revenue grew **20%** over the same period, to roughly **$750 million**.<sup>[1]</sup>

Those two growth rates are the most important pair of numbers in the category right now. Downloads grew seven times faster than in-app spending. Microdrama apps are finding new viewers far more quickly than they are turning those viewers into paying customers.

| Measure (Sensor Tower) | Value | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Global downloads, Q1 2026 | 850M+ | +140% YoY |
| In-app purchase revenue, Q1 2026 | ~$750M | +20% YoY |
| DramaBox in-app revenue, Q1 2026 | ~$140M | — |
| ReelShort in-app revenue, Q1 2026 | ~$140M | — |
| Average daily time spent, April 2026 | 25 min | +85% vs Jan 2025 |

### What one download is worth

A simple way to see the gap is to divide revenue by downloads. This is our arithmetic, not a figure Sensor Tower publishes, and it is rough: revenue in a quarter comes from everyone who already had the app, not only from that quarter's new installs. It is still a useful signal of direction.

- **Q1 2026:** about $750 million across more than 850 million downloads is **at most about $0.88** of in-app revenue per download.
- **Q1 2025:** working backwards from the growth rates, downloads were about 354 million (850 ÷ 2.4) and revenue about $625 million (750 ÷ 1.2). That is roughly **$1.77** per download.

On this rough measure, in-app revenue per download has about halved in a year. That fits what the regional figures below suggest: most of the new audience is arriving from markets where viewers spend less inside apps.

### Two apps take a large share of the money

DramaBox and ReelShort remained the top-grossing short drama apps in Q1 2026, each with close to **$140 million** in in-app revenue.<sup>[1]</sup> Together that is about $280 million, or a little more than a third of the category's roughly $750 million in in-app spending for the quarter. The download boom is broad, but the spending is concentrated in a small number of established apps.

## Where the new viewers are

Most of the growth in downloads is coming from outside the United States. In Q1 2026, Southeast Asia accounted for **32%** of short drama downloads, Latin America **23%** and India **22%**.<sup>[1]</sup> Those three regions together made up 77% of new installs. Everywhere else, including the US, Europe, Japan and Korea, shared the remaining 23%.

Adjust, which measures app installs from a different set of data, reports the same direction of travel. Its June 2026 report counted **2.3 billion** short drama app downloads in 2025, and said that by Q4 2025 downloads had increased **186%** year on year.<sup>[3]</sup>

Adjust's full-year total and Sensor Tower's quarterly total come from different panels and methods, so we do not add them together or treat one as a continuation of the other. What matters is that two independent measurement firms describe the same pattern: very fast download growth through 2025 and into 2026.

## Where the money is

Download share and revenue share are very different maps. Omdia, which counts all microdrama revenue rather than only app-store purchases, estimates that global microdrama revenue reached **$11 billion** in 2025 and will grow to **$14 billion** by the end of 2026.<sup>[2]</sup>

Omdia says **$3 billion** of the 2026 total will be generated outside China, and that the US is now the largest international market. By 2026, it expects the US to account for **50%** of all microdrama revenue outside China, reaching **$1.5 billion**.<sup>[2]</sup>

Putting Omdia's figures side by side:

| Market (Omdia, 2026 estimate) | Revenue | Share of global |
|---|---|---|
| Global | $14B | 100% |
| China | about $11B | about 79% |
| Outside China | $3B | about 21% |
| United States | $1.5B | about 11% |

The China and share figures in that table are our subtraction and division from Omdia's published numbers. They show how the format's economics still rest mostly on its home market, while the US carries half of the international business on its own.

Set against the download data, the contrast is sharp. The fastest-growing audiences are in Southeast Asia, Latin America and India, while the largest international revenue pool is in the United States. For anyone building in this category, the question is whether the new audiences will start to pay, or whether they will be served mainly through advertising and other models that app-store figures do not capture.

## Time spent: microdrama against streaming

Engagement is where microdrama makes its strongest case. Omdia reported US mobile viewing time per user per day for Q4 2025:<sup>[2]</sup>

| App (US, mobile, Q4 2025) | Minutes per user per day |
|---|---|
| ReelShort | 35.7 |
| Amazon Prime Video | 26.9 |
| Netflix | 24.8 |
| Disney+ | 23.0 |

On mobile, people who use ReelShort spend more time in it each day than people who use any of the three major streamers spend in theirs.

Sensor Tower's global figure measures something different: the average daily time spent across short drama apps. It reached **25 minutes** by April 2026, **up 85%** from January 2025, while time spent in OTT streaming apps stayed broadly flat at around **35 minutes**.<sup>[1]</sup> Working backwards, the category average was about 13.5 minutes a day in January 2025, so it has nearly doubled in about fifteen months.

These two engagement figures should not be compared directly. Omdia's is one leading app in one country on one platform. Sensor Tower's is an average across many apps worldwide. Read together, they say the same thing in two ways: the leading apps already hold viewers longer than mobile streaming does, and the category as a whole is closing the gap quickly.

## Why market-size numbers disagree

You will see very different totals for "the microdrama market". Usually they are not in conflict. They measure different things.

- **Sensor Tower** counts in-app purchase revenue: what people spend inside apps through the app stores. Its Q1 2026 figure of about $750 million is a quarterly, app-store-only number.
- **Omdia** estimates total microdrama revenue, not only app-store purchases, and includes China. Its $14 billion is an annual estimate for the whole market.
- **Adjust** measures installs, not revenue, from its own measurement data.

These numbers are all useful, but they cannot be added together, subtracted from each other or drawn as one line on a chart. Multiplying Sensor Tower's quarter by four and comparing it with Omdia's year would be a mistake, because the two firms are counting different money. We quote each figure with its source and never mix them. The full rules are on our [methodology]({{ site.baseurl }}/methodology/) page.

## What to watch next

Three questions will decide how the next year of microdrama looks, and each can be answered with published data as it appears:

1. **Does revenue start to catch up with downloads?** If in-app revenue growth stays near 20% while downloads grow at triple-digit rates, revenue per download will keep falling, and the business will rely more on advertising.
2. **Do the new regions start to pay?** Southeast Asia, Latin America and India lead downloads. Whether their share of revenue rises will show whether the format can earn money there, not just attract viewers.
3. **Does time spent keep rising?** The category average nearly doubled in fifteen months but is still below streaming's roughly 35 minutes. If the gap keeps closing, microdrama becomes a direct competitor for the same viewing time.
